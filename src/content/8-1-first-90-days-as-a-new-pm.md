## **The New PM Who Tried to Fix Everything on Day Three**

A newly-hired PM I once mentored arrived at her new company with genuine enthusiasm and, admittedly, genuine competence from her previous role. By her third day, she'd identified what she believed were several significant strategic problems with the existing roadmap and sent a lengthy, detailed email to leadership outlining her recommended changes.

The response was polite but distant. Nobody implemented her suggestions. Worse, she noticed a subtle but real shift in how colleagues engaged with her afterward—slightly more guarded, slightly less inclined to include her in informal discussions where genuine influence often gets built.

She hadn't necessarily been _wrong_ in her analysis. But she'd fundamentally misunderstood the sequencing of trust-building and influence that new roles require. **You cannot skip the process of genuinely earning credibility and understanding context, no matter how strong your prior experience or how confident your initial analysis feels.** This chapter provides a practical framework for navigating this crucial early period effectively.

---

## **Week 1-4: Learning Mode**

**Primary objective:** Absorb context, build relationships, and resist the urge to immediately demonstrate value through visible action or strong opinions.

**Practical priorities:**

**Genuine listening tours with key stakeholders.** Schedule individual conversations with engineering leads, designers, other PMs, customer-facing teams, and (if accessible) actual users—not to pitch your own ideas, but genuinely to understand their perspective on current challenges, history, and unstated context that isn't visible from job descriptions or onboarding documents.

**Understand the genuine "why" behind current practices**, applying Chesterton's Fence from Chapter 19 explicitly—before assuming any existing process or decision is simply outdated or poorly reasoned, genuinely investigate why it exists in its current form.

**Map the actual organizational dynamics**, applying Chapter 12's stakeholder influence/interest framework to your new specific context—who has genuine decision authority, who has informal influence beyond their formal title, and what are the genuine (not just officially stated) priorities driving current work.

**Resist premature strong opinions or dramatic recommendations.** Even if you notice genuine problems (and you likely will), recognize that your current understanding is necessarily incomplete, and that credibility for eventually addressing these problems requires first demonstrating genuine understanding of context and building relationship trust.

---

## **Week 5-8: Contributing Mode**

**Primary objective:** Begin genuine, visible contribution calibrated to your now-improved understanding of context, while continuing to build relationships and credibility.

**Practical priorities:**

**Identify and execute on "quick wins"**—genuinely valuable, relatively low-risk contributions that demonstrate competence and build momentum without requiring the kind of dramatic, high-stakes bets that risk significant credibility if they don't succeed.

**Begin sharing observations and questions, not just conclusions.** Rather than "this process is broken and should be replaced," try "I've noticed X pattern—can you help me understand the reasoning behind the current approach, and whether this has been a consideration before?" This invites collaborative dialogue rather than positioning yourself as an outside critic.

**Continue deepening relationships**, particularly with people whose work directly intersects with yours, moving from initial "getting to know you" conversations toward genuine, ongoing working relationships built through actual collaborative work.

---

## **Week 9-12: Leading Mode**

**Primary objective:** Begin exercising genuine, earned influence on strategic direction, backed by the credibility and context you've built over the preceding two months.

**Practical priorities:**

**Introduce more substantial ideas or recommendations**, now grounded in genuine understanding of context and backed by relationship credibility that makes your input more likely to be genuinely heard and considered, rather than dismissed as uninformed outside perspective.

**Take ownership of specific, defined areas of responsibility**, demonstrating your ability to drive genuine outcomes independently, building the track record that Chapter 2 identified as fundamental to PM authority.

**Begin mentoring or supporting others where appropriate**, even in a new role—sharing genuine expertise or perspective from your previous experience, now calibrated to your improved understanding of what's actually relevant and applicable to this specific context.

---

## **Relationship-Building Checklist**

Beyond the phased timeline above, certain relationship-building priorities deserve explicit, deliberate attention throughout your first 90 days:

☐ Have you had genuine, dedicated time with your direct manager to understand their expectations, working style, and how they prefer to communicate/collaborate?

☐ Have you built genuine rapport with your core engineering and design partners, understanding their working style and current concerns, not just their formal role?

☐ Have you identified who the genuine informal influencers are in your organization (Chapter 12), beyond formal title/position?

☐ Have you connected with peer PMs, both for genuine collaborative relationship-building and for candid, off-the-record context about organizational dynamics that might not surface in official onboarding?

☐ Have you found at least one genuine mentor or trusted colleague you can candidly discuss challenges and uncertainties with, rather than needing to project constant confidence?

## **Quick Wins Strategy: Choosing Genuinely Valuable, Low-Risk Contributions**

**What makes a good "quick win" candidate:**

- Genuinely valuable (solves a real, acknowledged problem, not just something that happens to be easy)
- Relatively low political complexity (doesn't require navigating significant, entrenched stakeholder disagreement)
- Achievable within a timeframe that allows visible demonstration of competence within your first two-three months
- Doesn't require you to have already built extensive organizational context/credibility to execute successfully

**What to avoid:**

- Attempting to tackle the most visible, celebrated strategic challenge immediately, before you've built adequate context and credibility to navigate its likely complexity
- Choosing something purely for visibility/impressiveness rather than genuine value, which experienced colleagues will likely recognize as somewhat performative
- Taking on something that requires significant cross-functional coordination before you've built the relationship foundation to navigate that coordination effectively

## **Chapter Summary: The TL;DR**

The first 90 days in a new PM role require deliberate sequencing: genuine learning and relationship-building (weeks 1-4) before visible contribution (weeks 5-8) before exercising more substantial strategic influence (weeks 9-12)—attempting to skip ahead to dramatic strategic recommendations before earning genuine context and credibility typically backfires, regardless of how strong your prior experience or how correct your initial analysis might actually be.

Deliberately prioritize relationship-building across key stakeholders throughout this period, apply Chesterton's Fence before assuming existing practices are simply outdated, and choose "quick win" contributions based on genuine value and appropriate scope rather than pure visibility or impressiveness.

This measured, patient approach to establishing yourself isn't about excessive caution or unnecessary deference—it's about recognizing that genuine, lasting influence requires a foundation of earned trust and contextual understanding that cannot be shortcut, regardless of how capable or well-intentioned you are.
