## **The Standup That Everyone Dreaded (And Nobody Would Admit It)**

I once inherited a team where the daily standup had ballooned into a 45-minute ritual that everyone privately hated but nobody had ever formally questioned. When I finally asked directly, in a 1:1, whether people found it valuable, one engineer admitted: "Honestly, I mentally check out after the first ten minutes. I already know what everyone's working on from Slack. This just feels like a status theater performance for whoever happens to be watching."

That comment stuck with me because it named something important: **rituals that once served a genuine purpose can calcify into empty performance long after the original need has changed or disappeared entirely, and organizational inertia keeps them running anyway, since questioning an established ritual can feel more disruptive than simply enduring it.**

This chapter isn't about prescribing a single "correct" set of rituals—we've already covered specific practices (standups, retros, sprint planning) in Chapter 9. Instead, it's about the meta-skill of periodically evaluating whether your team's rituals genuinely serve their intended purpose, and adjusting deliberately rather than defaulting to inertia.

---

## **The Core Diagnostic Question for Any Ritual**

Before examining specific ritual types, apply this simple, powerful diagnostic to any recurring meeting or practice: **"If we stopped doing this tomorrow, what specifically would break, and would anyone actually notice within two weeks?"**

If you genuinely can't answer this with something concrete and specific, that's valuable information suggesting the ritual may have drifted from serving genuine purpose into simple habit.

---

## **Daily Standups: When They Genuinely Help vs When They're Theater**

**Genuine value:** Quick synchronization on blockers and immediate priorities, particularly valuable for teams with high interdependency where daily coordination genuinely prevents wasted effort or duplicated work.

**When standups become theater:** When team members already have sufficient visibility into each other's work through other channels (Slack updates, shared project tracking tools), when the meeting consistently runs long without corresponding value, or when it becomes a status-reporting ritual to a manager rather than genuine peer coordination.

**Practical recalibration options:** Consider async standups (written updates in Slack rather than synchronous meetings) for teams with strong written communication habits and lower interdependency needs, or reduce frequency (2-3x weekly rather than daily) if genuine coordination needs don't require daily synchronization.

---

## **Weekly Team Syncs: Balancing Information-Sharing and Actual Discussion**

**Genuine value:** Broader alignment beyond daily tactical coordination—reviewing weekly progress against goals, surfacing emerging risks or blockers, and maintaining shared context across a slightly longer time horizon than daily standups address.

**Common dysfunction:** Becoming a passive status-reporting ritual where each person shares updates while others only partially listen, waiting for their own turn, rather than genuine collaborative discussion.

**A healthier structure:** Distinguish between information that can be shared asynchronously beforehand (written updates people read independently) versus genuine discussion topics that warrant synchronous meeting time—reserving the actual meeting for discussion, decisions, and problem-solving rather than passive reporting that could have been an email.

---

## **Monthly Business Reviews: Connecting Tactical Work to Strategic Outcomes**

**Genuine value:** Regular, deliberate connection between day-to-day execution and broader strategic goals and business outcomes—preventing the drift where tactical work continues without periodic honest assessment of whether it's genuinely driving intended impact.

**Common dysfunction:** Becoming a purely backward-looking reporting exercise (what we did) without genuine forward-looking strategic discussion (what this means for our direction going forward) or honest acknowledgment of underperformance against goals.

**A healthier structure:** Explicitly separate "what happened" reporting from "what should we do differently" strategic discussion, ensuring the review genuinely influences forward decision-making rather than existing purely as retrospective documentation.

---

## **Quarterly Planning: Avoiding the Theater of Ritual Consensus**

We covered quarterly planning process in depth in Chapter 7, but it's worth reinforcing here as a ritual specifically vulnerable to becoming theater: elaborate planning ceremonies that produce impressive-looking roadmap documents without genuine, rigorous prioritization reasoning or realistic capacity assessment underneath the polished presentation.

**The diagnostic question applied here:** Does your quarterly planning process genuinely change what gets built, or does it primarily formalize decisions that were effectively already made informally beforehand, functioning more as ceremonial validation than genuine strategic decision-making?

## **User Research Sessions: Maintaining Genuine Rigor Over Time**

**Genuine value:** Ongoing connection to actual user needs and behavior, preventing the drift toward internally-generated assumptions disconnected from genuine user reality (Chapter 4's core discovery principles).

**Common dysfunction:** Research sessions that become confirmation-seeking rituals (Chapter 4's "research theater") rather than genuine, open discovery, particularly as teams become more confident and established, potentially losing some of the genuine curiosity and openness to disconfirmation that characterized earlier-stage discovery work.

**A healthy practice:** Periodically and explicitly ask, even for well-established research practices: "Are we still genuinely open to being surprised by what we learn, or have these sessions become validation exercises for decisions we've effectively already made?"

## **A Practical Ritual Audit Process**

Consider conducting a genuine, periodic (perhaps quarterly or semi-annually) audit of your team's full set of recurring rituals:

**1. List every recurring meeting/ritual**, including frequency and typical duration.

**2. Apply the core diagnostic question** to each: what would break if this stopped, and would anyone notice within two weeks?

**3. Genuinely solicit team input**, not just your own assessment—team members experiencing rituals as theater versus genuine value may have perspective that isn't visible from a purely facilitator or leadership viewpoint.

**4. Experiment with adjustments** (reduced frequency, format changes, or elimination) rather than assuming any specific change is permanent—treat ritual adjustment itself as an experiment subject to genuine evaluation, not just a one-time decision.

**5. Revisit this audit periodically**, since genuine ritual needs shift as team size, maturity, and context change over time—a ritual that was genuinely valuable at one stage may become theater at a later stage, or vice versa.

## **Chapter Summary: The TL;DR**

Recurring rituals (standups, weekly syncs, business reviews, quarterly planning, retrospectives, research sessions) can drift from genuine value into empty performance over time, particularly as original context and needs shift while the ritual itself persists through organizational inertia.

Apply the core diagnostic question—"if we stopped this tomorrow, what would break, and would anyone notice within two weeks?"—periodically to every recurring practice, rather than assuming established rituals automatically continue serving genuine purpose indefinitely.

Conduct genuine, periodic ritual audits incorporating honest team input, experimenting with adjustments rather than treating any specific ritual structure as permanently fixed, and recognizing that genuine ritual needs evolve as team size, maturity, and context change over time.
