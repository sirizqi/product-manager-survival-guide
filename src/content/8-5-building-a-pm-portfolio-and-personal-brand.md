## **The Best PM Nobody Had Heard Of**

I once worked alongside a genuinely exceptional PM—call her Wren—whose product judgment, discovery rigor, and stakeholder navigation skills were, by any honest assessment, better than most senior leaders I'd encountered. She consistently drove genuine impact, mentored junior colleagues generously, and made consistently sound strategic calls.

When she went looking for her next role after five years at the same company, she struggled. Not because her skills weren't genuinely excellent—they were. But she had no documented case studies, no visible professional presence beyond her immediate team's awareness, and no way to translate five years of genuinely strong work into something a prospective employer could evaluate beyond a fairly generic resume and vague interview answers about "driving results."

Meanwhile, PMs with objectively less impressive track records, but genuine skill in documenting and communicating their work, moved more fluidly into strong opportunities—not because they were better at the actual job, but because they'd invested in making their genuine competence _visible and evaluable_ to people who hadn't personally witnessed it firsthand.

This chapter isn't about manufacturing false impressiveness—it's about ensuring genuine competence doesn't remain invisible simply because you assumed good work speaks for itself. Often, it genuinely doesn't, at least not to people outside your immediate working context.

---

## **Documenting Your Work: Building the Raw Material**

**The habit to build now, not later:** Keep an ongoing, private record of specific projects, decisions, and outcomes as you work—not reconstructed months later from memory when you suddenly need it for a job search, but captured close to when it actually happened, while details and genuine reasoning are still fresh.

**What genuinely valuable documentation includes:**

- The specific problem/context you were addressing
- Your genuine reasoning process (not just the final decision)
- Concrete, quantified outcomes where possible
- Honest acknowledgment of what you'd do differently (this often demonstrates more genuine skill than a purely polished success narrative)

---

## **Case Studies: Translating Work Into Evaluable Narrative**

A genuine case study, whether for a portfolio, interview preparation, or professional writing, should follow a structure that reveals genuine thinking, not just outcomes:

**Context:** What was the situation, and why did it matter?

**Your specific role and approach:** What did you specifically do, think through, and decide—distinguishing your individual contribution from broader team effort where relevant?

**Genuine challenges/trade-offs faced:** What made this genuinely difficult, and how did you navigate that difficulty?

**Outcome:** What happened, ideally with honest, specific metrics rather than vague claims?

**Reflection:** What would you do differently, and what did this teach you that applies beyond this specific instance?

**Why this structure matters:** It demonstrates the genuine thinking process this entire book has emphasized—not just "I shipped X and it worked," but the reasoning, trade-offs, and honest reflection that reveal genuine product judgment.

---

## **Public Speaking and Writing: Building Visible Thought Leadership**

**Why this matters practically:** Beyond personal satisfaction, genuine public contribution (conference talks, thoughtful articles, panel participation) creates visible evidence of your thinking that extends beyond people who've directly worked with you—addressing exactly the "invisibility" problem Wren faced.

**Practical starting points:**

- Writing about genuine lessons learned from specific work (anonymized/generalized as needed for confidentiality) rather than generic, widely-available advice
- Speaking at internal company events before pursuing external opportunities, building genuine comfort and refined content before higher-stakes external visibility
- Contributing thoughtfully to PM community discussions (forums, LinkedIn, industry Slack communities) rather than purely consuming content passively

**The genuine authenticity test:** Does your public content reflect genuine, specific thinking and experience, or does it recycle generic industry advice indistinguishable from thousands of similar posts? The former builds genuine credibility; the latter adds noise without meaningfully differentiating your actual expertise.

---

## **LinkedIn Presence: Practical, Not Performative**

**What genuinely helps:** Clear, specific descriptions of your actual scope and impact in each role (not just job titles), occasional genuine, substantive posts sharing real professional insight or reflection, and authentic engagement with others' content that reflects genuine professional interest rather than purely transactional networking.

**What often backfires:** Purely performative "humble brag" content, generic motivational content indistinguishable from thousands of similar posts, or a presence that feels more focused on appearing impressive than genuinely, substantively useful to your professional network.

---

## **Contributing to the PM Community**

Beyond personal brand-building, genuine community contribution—mentoring other PMs, participating in industry discussions, sharing genuine lessons (including failures, as emphasized throughout this book)—serves both the community and, as a genuine byproduct rather than primary motivation, your own visibility and professional network.

**A practical starting point:** Identify one specific way you could contribute this quarter—mentoring a junior PM outside your immediate team, writing one genuinely substantive reflection on a specific challenge you've navigated, or participating meaningfully in a relevant professional community—rather than treating "build my brand" as a vague, perpetually deferred aspiration.

## **Chapter Summary: The TL;DR**

Genuine competence doesn't automatically translate into visible, evaluable professional credibility—documenting your work as you go, building genuine case studies that reveal thinking process rather than just outcomes, and investing in authentic public contribution (writing, speaking, community participation) helps ensure strong work doesn't remain invisible to people outside your immediate working context.

Prioritize authentic, specific content over generic, performative professional presence—genuine reflection and substantive insight build lasting credibility, while purely performative content adds noise without meaningfully differentiating genuine expertise.
